When you need help with anxiety, stress or a period that has knocked you off balance, the first search is almost always "psychologist Cluj". And the results mix together words that do not mean the same thing: psychologist, psychotherapist, counselor, coach, psychiatrist.

> "Psychologist or psychotherapist — is there really a difference?"

Yes, and the difference is not just vocabulary. These words describe a person's training, the title they are allowed to use by law, and the kind of help you actually get in the room. And if your question is the concrete one — which of them do I go to? — the answer isn't decided by the title, but by what you need.

## What each one means in Romania

- **Psychologist** — a title protected by law. In Romania you can practice psychology only if you are a member of the Romanian College of Psychologists (CPR) and hold the right to private practice. That right comes after specialized studies and after completing the steps required by the CPR, not with a bachelor's degree.
- **Psychotherapist** — this title does not come automatically with a degree either. The CPR grants it after accredited training in a therapeutic model, which includes supervision and the therapist's own personal therapy. That is why we meet psychologists who are not psychotherapists — and psychotherapists whose background is in another specialization.
- **Clinical psychologist** — a psychologist with a specialization in clinical psychology, recognized by the CPR. This is the title you most often see inside the healthcare system: hospitals, clinical psychology offices, psychological assessment.
- **Counselor or coach** — fields where the profession is not regulated in the same way. That does not mean there are no good practitioners there; it means the title itself gives you no guarantee about the person's training.
- **Psychiatrist** — a medical doctor. Can make a psychiatric diagnosis and can prescribe medication. Neither a psychologist nor a psychotherapist can prescribe. When medication is needed, psychotherapy and psychiatry work together, not one instead of the other.

## Why the confusion is normal

The word you type usually describes what you feel, not the profession you need. When something is wrong you look for a "psychologist", because that is the word you know. The service you actually need may be psychotherapy.

This is exactly why the searches "psychologist Cluj" and "psychotherapist Cluj" so often lead to the same person. I am a psychologist with the right to private practice in Cognitive and Behavioral Psychotherapies, accredited in the public CPR register — so both words apply to the same work.

## Psychologist or psychotherapist: when each one fits

- **When you need a psychologist** — you need assessment and testing: a clear psychological profile, a neuropsychological assessment, a report for school or for court. Or you need counselling for a difficult stretch: a loss, a decision, a period of stress that doesn't lift.
- **When you need a psychotherapist** — something has been repeating for months and keeps you stuck: anxiety, panic, anger that explodes, relationships that unfold the same way, a pattern you can see but can't stop. Psychotherapy works with the mechanism that keeps the problem alive, not only with the event that started it.
- **When you don't know** — and that is the most common answer. You call and ask. A serious professional doesn't sell you a session you don't need: either they take you on, or they tell you who fits what you have.

It isn't a choice made once and for all. Many people start with a psychologist and continue with a psychotherapist; others need both, in parallel, plus a psychiatrist when sleep or the body is involved.

## What is worth asking before the first session

- **Which register is this person in?** — psychologists with the right to private practice appear in the public CPR register. A serious practice answers this question without hesitating.
- **Which approach do you use?** — CBT and REBT have a defined protocol and can be described in a few sentences. If the approach cannot be explained to you, you cannot judge the progress either.
- **How long does this kind of work usually take?** — an honest answer includes an estimate and the conditions under which the estimate changes.
- **How do we know it is working?** — goals are set concretely at the beginning and reviewed. If nothing moves after a few sessions, the plan is re-evaluated, not extended indefinitely.
- **What changes if we work online?** — in online sessions the goals and the exercises are the same; what changes is the setting, not the method.
