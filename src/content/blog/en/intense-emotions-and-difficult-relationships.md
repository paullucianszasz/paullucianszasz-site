Are you tired of the intensity of your own emotions? Or do you find yourself, year after year, in the same painful patterns with other people, even though this time you promised yourself it would be different?

Sometimes what we struggle with does not look like one specific problem, but like a constant, exhausting way of relating to ourselves and to the world. It is exactly the kind of suffering people describe like this:

> "It isn't one thing that is wrong. It is how I function."

## The patterns I see most often

- **Emotions that feel hard to control** — fast shifts from calm to anger, deep sadness or overwhelming anxiety, sometimes set off by details that look small from the outside.
- **Patterns that repeat in relationships** — an intense fear of rejection or of loss, the need to control everything in order to feel safe, or the opposite: withdrawing suddenly when someone gets too close.
- **A strained relationship with yourself** — a very harsh inner critic, a persistent sense of inner emptiness, or the feeling that you don't really know who you are or what you want.
- **Reactions in the moment, regret afterwards** — impulsive decisions made on a background of stress or pain, which later bring guilt and misunderstanding from the people around you.

These reactions don't mean you are a "difficult" person. Most of the time they are protective mechanisms learned long ago, which today consume your energy and make the present heavier.

## What these patterns have in common

None of them appears out of nowhere. Take a single episode and take it apart, and the same chain usually shows up:

- **A small trigger** — a tone of voice, a delay, a message left unanswered, the other person's need for space.
- **An automatic interpretation** — "they're angry with me", "they're about to leave", "if I stand out, I risk being humiliated".
- **An intense emotion** — anger, panic, shame, emptiness. It isn't exaggerated: it is proportionate to the interpretation, not to the situation.
- **A protective behavior** — reproach, control, silence, disappearing, excessive apologies, a decision made on the spot.
- **Short relief, followed by regret** — the tension drops, but the consequences stay, and the list grows.

The protection worked once. That is exactly why it repeats: it stays alive because it brought relief then, not because it still works now.

This chain is described step by step in [the ABC model](/en/blog/understanding-how-therapy-works-the-abc-model/), with one difference: here it doesn't appear once, but dozens of times a week, in the relationships that matter.

## The working relationship is what changes things

With this kind of difficulty, information is not the problem. Most people who come to me already know that the reaction was out of proportion. Knowing doesn't stop the reaction.

What can stop it is an experience lived in relationship with another person, not a text to read: that someone stays calm after you exploded, that they don't punish you for what you felt, that they don't disappear when you set a limit. Without that experience, what remains is one more theory about your patterns.

So here, structure is in service of the relationship, not the other way around. If the working relationship isn't solid, no technique holds; if it is solid, the technique has somewhere to work.

## What happens between us is material for the work

A pattern that repeats in all of your relationships will, at some point, appear in the relationship with me too. You may test how seriously I take what you say, you may feel rejected by something I said, you may want to stop coming for a few weeks.

That is the best moment of the session, not a failure of it. We can stop and look at what is happening between us, live, without either of us ending up as the guilty one. What can be seen there is usually identical to what happens at home.

## What the work looks like in practice

- **We start with what you bring, not with an agenda I impose** — one episode, taken apart step by step. A pattern is easier to see in one concrete case than in ten years of history.
- **Regulation before any decision** — while the emotion is at its maximum there is no conversation, there is only defense. Before changing anything in a relationship, we learn to catch the moment the tension rises.
- **We examine the rules nobody discusses** — the rigid demands about how others "must" treat you and how you "must" be in order to be accepted. REBT works exactly here, not only on the surface behavior.
- **We practice between sessions, at your pace** — the pattern doesn't appear in my office. It appears on Sunday evening, in the car, on a phone call. I don't give you something to work on that you aren't ready to work on.

## How structured it will actually be

The framework I work in is **CBT and REBT**: the event, the interpretation, the emotion, the demands underneath the anger or underneath the panic. I name these out loud, because otherwise they stay invisible. The rest of the form — how much agenda, how many exercises, how fast — is not set in advance. It is chosen together, session by session.

With this kind of suffering, too much structure too quickly can become a problem in itself: one more experience in which you are asked to conform, in which what you feel is not taken seriously, in which the pace belongs to someone else. If you have been through a therapy where you were handed worksheets to fill in and you left feeling untouched, then you are right to look skeptically at any method.

So in the difficult sessions, the agenda can wait. Sometimes the real work is simply staying with what happened yesterday, without rushing anything and without turning it into homework. What is missing is not direction: we know what we are aiming at, and we check that together, regularly.

Work like this takes time. Not because someone insists, but because trust builds over time, and it shows in the harder moments, not in the easy ones. If I feel the work isn't helping, I tell you: good work has conversations about how it is going, not only continuations.
